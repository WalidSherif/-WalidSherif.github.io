About

Engineering Focus

I am a Renewable Energy Engineering undergraduate at Zewail City University whose work has progressively developed into power-systems engineering: load-flow and contingency analysis, grid-aware placement of large data-center loads, and mixed-integer optimization on transmission-scale networks.

My core research asks one question at increasing scale: where can large data-center loads be connected to a power grid without violating voltage and thermal limits? It progressed from a 50 MW-class load on the IEEE 33-bus and IEEE 118-bus test systems, through GA/PSO metaheuristics and stochastic problem formulations, to a mixed-integer nonlinear optimization of 13 candidate data-center sites on a 2,891-bus Virginia transmission network — validated with AC power flow and N-1 contingency analysis.

Alongside this research, I maintain breadth across power electronics and harmonics, wind-turbine aerodynamics and CFD, thermal management, and infrastructure engineering from my internship on the Al Hudayriat Island development in Abu Dhabi.

Flagship Research

Grid-Aware Data-Center Placement on the Virginia Power Network

Large-scale data-center allocation on a 2,891-bus, 1,833-line Virginia electrical network, formulated as a mixed-integer nonlinear planning problem and solved through hierarchical MILP/MISOCP decomposition, with full AC power-flow and N-1 contingency validation.

From a 50 MW load to a 2,891-bus transmission network

Large data-center developments introduce substantial new electrical demand, making siting and grid-feasibility decisions increasingly important at transmission scale. Connecting large facilities can affect voltage and line loading; allocating demand across thirteen candidate sites becomes a mixed-integer nonlinear planning problem with discrete siting decisions, continuous MW/MVAr allocations, and nonlinear AC power-flow physics.

The final workflow couples a SCIP assignment MILP with a second-order-cone (SOC) branch-flow optimization per electrical island, then accepts no result until it passes full AC power-flow validation in pandapower and a 225-contingency N-1 study.

Research Journey

  1. Stage 1

    50 MW Data Center — IEEE 33-Bus

    Problem definition and feeder screening: power flow, 0.95–1.05 pu voltage constraints, candidate-bus analysis, and electrical feasibility for large-load placement on the 33-bus distribution feeder.

  2. Stage 2

    IEEE 118-Bus Transmission System

    Full enumeration of 118 candidate buses under voltage and line-loading limits, with per-bus hosting-capacity sweeps up to 285.6 MW at the strongest bus.

  3. Stage 3

    GA / PSO — Metaheuristic Optimization

    Genetic Algorithm and Particle Swarm Optimization search for grid-feasible placement. Both converge to the same bus as exhaustive enumeration — cross-validation, not a global-optimality claim.

  4. Stage 4

    PJM Stochastic Optimization

    Extension from deterministic test cases toward uncertainty-aware planning motivated by PJM-scale data-center growth — the formulation groundwork for large-scale allocation.

  5. Stage 5

    Virginia Grid — 2,891 Buses / 1,833 Lines

    Synthetic Virginia transmission network with NREL county-level data-center capacity data; 13 candidate sites totaling 11,559.4 MW; the network fragments into approximately 608 electrical islands.

  6. Stage 6

    Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Planning — MILP / MISOCP Decomposition

    Discrete site/island/bus/assignment decisions + continuous MW/MVAr allocation + nonlinear AC power-flow physics form a large-scale mixed-integer nonlinear planning problem. Solved by hierarchical decomposition — a global assignment MILP using SCIP via PySCIPOpt, followed by an MISOCP per electrical island using a Jabr SOC branch-flow relaxation. This is not a monolithic MINLP solver. Gurobi was evaluated but unavailable; the final stack is open-source.

  7. Stage 7

    AC + N-1 Validation

    Optimization → candidate feasibility → AC power flow → voltage/line-loading checks → final-placement validation → 225-contingency N-1 study. No optimization output was accepted without passing the electrical validation checks.

  8. Final Result

    1,258.8 MW Allocated

    Under a source-constrained scenario, with no additional external import assumed, the existing generation fleet supports 1,258.8 MW of the 11,559.4 MW requested across 7 accepted sites, with zero new violations relative to the validated baseline.
