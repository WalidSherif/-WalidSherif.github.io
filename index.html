Research

Grid-Aware Data-Center Placement

One question, studied at increasing scale and rigor: where can large data-center loads be connected to a power grid while keeping voltages and line loadings within limits?

From a 50 MW load to a 2,891-bus transmission network

Data centers are concentrating in grids such as Virginia's faster than any conventional load. Connecting a single large facility degrades voltages and line loadings; connecting thirteen at county scale becomes a mixed-integer nonlinear planning problem with discrete siting decisions, continuous MW/MVAr allocations, and nonlinear AC power-flow physics.

The final workflow couples a SCIP assignment MILP with a second-order-cone (SOC) branch-flow optimization per electrical island, then accepts no result until it passes full AC power-flow validation in pandapower and a 225-contingency N-1 study.

Research Progression

  1. Stage 1

    50 MW Data Center — Problem Definition

    Where can a large data-center load be connected while maintaining acceptable grid operating conditions? Voltage, thermal, and feasibility constraints defined.

  2. Stage 2

    IEEE 33-Bus Distribution Feeder

    Power-flow analysis on the 33-bus test feeder: voltage constraints, candidate-bus screening, and hosting-capacity concepts for large loads.

  3. Stage 3

    IEEE 118-Bus Transmission System

    Full enumeration of 118 candidate buses with 0.95–1.05 pu voltage limits, line-loading limits, and per-bus hosting-capacity sweeps (up to 285.6 MW at the strongest bus).

  4. Stage 4

    GA / PSO Metaheuristics

    Genetic Algorithm and Particle Swarm Optimization search for grid-feasible placement; both converge to the same bus as exhaustive search, cross-validating the formulation.

  5. Stage 5

    Toward Stochastic Optimization (PJM Context)

    Extension from deterministic test cases toward uncertainty-aware planning motivated by PJM-scale data-center growth; formulation groundwork for large-scale allocation.

  6. Stage 6

    Virginia Grid — 2,891 Buses / 1,833 Lines

    Synthetic Virginia transmission network with NREL county-level data-center capacity data; 13 candidate sites totaling 11,559.4 MW; network fragments into ~608 electrical islands.

  7. Stage 7

    Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Optimization

    Discrete site/island/bus selection + continuous MW allocation + nonlinear AC constraints → three-stage hierarchical decomposition: assignment MILP (SCIP/PySCIPOpt) then MISOCP per island (Jabr SOC branch-flow relaxation).

  8. Stage 8

    AC Power-Flow Validation

    Every accepted candidate re-verified with pandapower Newton-Raphson: 1,992 feasible site–island pairs screened; 68 candidates AC-benchmarked (64 feasible, 4 infeasible); SOC vs AC Spearman ρ = 1.0000, loss error ≤ 0.07%.

  9. Stage 9

    N-1 Contingency Analysis

    225 branch and generator contingencies simulated: all converge, zero new voltage or thermal violations versus baseline, worst post-contingency loading 99.35%.

  10. Final Result

    1,258.8 MW Allocated

    Under a source-constrained scenario (no additional external import), the existing generation fleet serves 1,258.8 MW of the 11,559.4 MW requested across 7 accepted sites — with zero new violations versus baseline.
